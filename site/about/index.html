CA Property Tax per acre

Map Highlights

About this map

This map shows annual property tax revenue per acre of land for each parcel. Tax per acre is a measure of how productively land generates public revenue: a downtown apartment building and a surface parking lot may occupy similar land, but can differ in tax yield by a factor of 100 or more.

Why taxes vary so much: Proposition 13

Under California's Proposition 13 (1978), property is taxed at roughly 1% of its assessed value, and assessed value is set at the purchase price and can grow at most 2% per year until the property is sold. Because market values have grown much faster than 2%, two identical neighboring homes can pay wildly different taxes depending on when they were last sold. This map makes those disparities — and the low yield of underused land — visible.

Methodology

  • Tax data: county tax rolls scraped in January 2024 by the ca-property-tax project (officialdata.org).
  • Parcel boundaries: county GIS open data (San Francisco: DataSF). Lot area is computed from parcel polygons in an equal-area projection (California Albers, EPSG:3310).
  • Condominiums: each condo unit is its own tax parcel but shares one physical lot. Units are aggregated to the building footprint; the map shows the sum of all unit taxes divided by the lot's acreage, and popups note how many tax lots were combined.
  • Gray parcels had no matching record in the tax roll (e.g., recently re-numbered lots). Purple parcels pay $0 — typically government, religious, or nonprofit exemptions.
  • Tiny sliver lots (under ~30 sq ft) are excluded from the top/bottom rankings but still drawn on the map.

Caveats

  • Tax amounts include parcel taxes, special assessments, and Mello-Roos charges, not just the 1% ad-valorem levy.
  • The tax roll (Jan 2024) and the parcel map (current) are from different dates, so a small share of parcels don't match.
  • Zillow links are best-effort address searches, not verified matches.
